## Getting to Providence / The Department from Boston
---

The Brown CS department is located at [115 Waterman St, Providence, RI 02906](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Department+of+Computer+Science/@41.8225257,-71.4085214,16z/data=!4m10!1m2!2m1!1sbrown+cit!3m6!1s0x89e4453b5275469d:0xc48a812b6cfb273!8m2!3d41.826953!4d-71.3995786!15sCglicm93biBjaXSSARV1bml2ZXJzaXR5X2RlcGFydG1lbnTgAQA!16s%2Fg%2F1hhwdn5zz?entry=ttu).

**By Train**<br>
You can take the Northeast Regional Amtrak train from Back Bay or South Station to Providence. See details [here](https://www.amtrak.com/northeast-regional-train). The train takes about 30 minutes each way and costs about $70 round-trip. You can also take [the Acela](https://www.amtrak.com/acela-train) though it doesn't really save you much time between Boston and Providence as compared to the Northeast Regional.

You can also take the Providence/Stoughton commuter rail line from Back Bay or South Station to Providence. See details [here](https://www.mbta.com/schedules/CR-Providence/timetable?schedule_direction[direction_id]=1#direction-filter). The train takes about an hour each way and costs about $25 round-trip.

The Brown CS department is about a 20 minute (uphill) walk or 5 minute drive/Uber from the [Providence train station](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Providence/@41.8292499,-71.4158576,17z/data=!3m1!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x89e4451a0a9b645b:0xba5fd5685df9b6b5!8m2!3d41.8292499!4d-71.4132827!16s%2Fg%2F1thqhs9w?entry=ttu).

**By Car**<br>
Driving takes about an hour. There is a [visitor parking lot](https://www.brown.edu/about/administration/transportation/parking/visitor-parking) at Brown, metered parking around the building and you can generally find free parking a few blocks east of the computer science department (along e.g. Governor Street). The city of Providence does not allow you to park overnight [unless you pay $100 for a car registered in Providence or $200 for a car registered outside of Providence](https://www.providenceri.gov/public-works/overnight-parking/) and, in theory, will ticket you if you park overnight in free residential parking.
